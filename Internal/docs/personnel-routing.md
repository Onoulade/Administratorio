# Personnel Routing

Research **Personnel Routing** after Yellow-Magenta Bureaucracy. It consumes Administrative, Utility (yellow), Agricultural (Gleba), and Electromagnetic (Fulgora) science. Deployment and Reception Offices each cost two Yellow-Magenta Forms, ten steel plates, and five advanced circuits. Signs also require Yellow-Magenta Forms. Operation requires neither salary nor power.

1. Place a **Personnel Deployment Office** and rotate its arrow toward the departure lane.
2. Place a **Personnel Routing Sign** at every turn and rotate its arrow toward the next sign or Reception Office. Offices and signs snap to the same native 2×2 grid as rails. Each segment runs straight on dry, unobstructed ground. A sign must find another sign or reception in its direction; a second deployment office blocks the connection. Cycles and routes over 512 blocks are rejected.
3. **Pavement appears automatically** between valid aligned destinations. The two-tile-wide teal-tinted vanilla concrete is a visual aid and protects the walking lane; there is no path item to craft or place. Every office and sign also supplies its own 2×2 pavement. Removing or rotating idle infrastructure updates connected lanes and restores the original ground where a lane is no longer needed. Existing manually placed path entities are replaced with automatic pavement during an update, without losing in-flight cargo.
4. End the route at a **Personnel Reception Office**. Its orientation does not affect reception.
5. Use an inserter to load personnel into the deployment office's source slot and another to collect arrivals from the reception office's output slots. The complete route must exist before dispatch. Hired Biter Field Agents, rideable biters, eggs, and ordinary cargo are not accepted. Other biter personnel, including trainees, specialists, logistics biters, managers, briefed managers, and egg couriers, are accepted.

Belts (including underground belts and splitters), inserters, and power poles may occupy the lane. Clear trees, rocks, cliffs, pipes, machines, and rails first. Other structures cannot be built on the protected pavement. Tiles cannot replace automatic pavement while its connection or node exists; remove or rotate the idle signs/offices first to restore the original ground.

Each biter owns a 2×2 block. Movement follows saved lane positions at vanilla small-biter speed (0.2 tiles/tick, quantized to the engine’s position resolution), with no stop at ordinary 2×2 blocks. A bounded look-ahead reserves free blocks and the preceding block remains held as a safety gap. Native units supply walking animation; script-controlled lane positions prevent pathfinding wobble, escape, and spurious halfway returns. Turns occur at sign centers. Animation commands target the end of the straight leg and do not stop at every block. Queues stop before occupied blocks and at full reception outputs. Merges give already admitted personnel priority in admission order. Conflicting uses of a shared block wait at deployment until the earlier journey finishes; they cannot create a head-on deadlock. Immediately neighboring independent lanes also wait for clearance. Leave one 2×2 block of space between independent parallel lanes for simultaneous traffic. A tightly folded route that brings distant legs beside each other carries one journey at a time; spread out its turns for convoy throughput.

Walking personnel have role-specific body tints. Existing item colors carry over for workers, trainees, and operations specialists; the remaining roles have their own colors. Ordinary managers are violet, while each briefing and courier type has a separate tint. Legacy worker items share the worker's green color. Body tint identifies the role loaded at departure. When a briefing expires, its badge and time bar disappear; reception returns the ordinary manager item. The body retains its departure tint until reception. Native sprite masks provide the color; body details, shadows, and worker helmets remain intact.

Signs and offices on an admitted route are protected from mining, rotation, and combat until its last journey finishes. Their automatic pavement stays protected until then. This intentionally protects the complete planned route, including signs farther ahead. To edit it, stop loading the deployment office, empty reception, and let the queue drain. Buildings become editable automatically afterward; pavement follows their connections.

The original item stays in a saved script inventory throughout transit. Its quality, health, and item metadata are retained; transport does not train personnel. Spoilage clocks continue normally, including while queued at a full output: expired briefings or couriers return their existing spoil result, without renewing the briefing or losing the manager. Briefed managers display a small briefing-material badge (gear, repair pack, form, circuit, or rocket fuel) and a shrinking remaining-time bar above their walking entity. The bar changes from green to amber to red as expiry approaches; the badge and bar disappear when the briefing expires. Save/load retains the overlay and its original spoilage clock. Reception commits the item to an output slot before releasing the proxy and its reservations. A full output retains the item and waits.

If infrastructure or a proxy is forcibly deleted by another script, or a claimed block becomes invalid, the walking proxy is removed and the original item returns to deployment. If deployment is unavailable or full, a **Personnel Recovery Crate** appears nearby and raises an alert. Retrieve its contents manually or with an inserter. If no crate can be placed, cargo remains in saved recovery storage and placement retries every five seconds. Surface deletion/clearing evacuates in-flight personnel to recovery crates at the force's Nauvis spawn. Save/load retains jobs, cargo, and reservations. Field Agents already traveling in a save from before their exclusion are recovered safely during the update.

There are no circuit controls on these offices. Control input with your inserter and drain the output to stop and edit a route. This system is independent of employment dispatch, Biterport logistics, and passenger trains.
