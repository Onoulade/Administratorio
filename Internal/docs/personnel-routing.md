# Personnel Routing

Research **Personnel Routing** after Yellow-Magenta Bureaucracy. It consumes Administrative, Utility (yellow), Agricultural (Gleba), and Electromagnetic (Fulgora) science. Deployment and Reception Offices each cost two Yellow-Magenta Forms, ten steel plates, and five advanced circuits. Signs also require Yellow-Magenta Forms. Operation requires neither salary nor power.

1. Place a **Personnel Deployment Office** and rotate its arrow toward the departure lane.
2. Place a **Personnel Routing Sign** at every turn and rotate its arrow toward the next sign or Reception Office. Offices and signs snap to the same native 2×2 grid as rails. Each segment runs straight on dry, unobstructed ground. A sign must find another sign or reception in its direction; a second deployment office blocks the connection. Each straight segment is limited to 512 blocks.
3. **Pavement appears automatically** between valid aligned destinations. The two-tile-wide teal-tinted vanilla concrete is a visual aid and protects the walking lane; there is no path item to craft or place. Every office and sign also supplies its own 2×2 pavement. Removing or rotating idle infrastructure updates connected lanes and restores the original ground where a lane is no longer needed. Existing manually placed path entities are replaced with automatic pavement during an update, without losing in-flight cargo.
4. End the route at a **Personnel Reception Office**. It cannot be rotated and accepts incoming paths from all four sides, including simultaneous arrivals; the shared entry queues them safely.
5. Use an inserter to load personnel into the deployment office's source slot and another to collect arrivals from the reception office's output slots. Only the next sign or reception must be reachable before dispatch. A reception office at the end of the entire journey is not required. Hired Biter Field Agents, rideable biters, eggs, and ordinary cargo are not accepted. Other biter personnel, including trainees, specialists, logistics biters, managers, briefed managers, and egg couriers, are accepted.

Belts (including underground belts and splitters), inserters, and power poles may occupy the lane. Clear trees, rocks, cliffs, pipes, machines, and rails first. Other structures cannot be built on the protected pavement. Tiles cannot replace automatic pavement while its connection or node exists; remove or rotate the idle signs/offices first to restore the original ground.

Each biter occupies a 2×2 body area. Movement follows saved lane positions at vanilla small-biter speed (0.2 tiles/tick, quantized to the engine’s position resolution), without stopping at ordinary 2×2 blocks. A spatial index keeps centers at least 2.5 tiles apart along shared lanes, leaving a small margin for the native animation proxy. Waiting queues fill the lane closely instead of retaining an extra empty block. Native units supply walking animation; saved positions prevent steering wobble, escape, and spurious halfway returns. Turns occur at sign centers.

Routes are admitted **one sign-to-sign segment at a time**. Personnel walk to the next valid sign even when no reception is reachable beyond it. Before a sign without a valid onward connection, personnel stop outside its 2×2 tile, normally with their centers 2.5 tiles before its center; followers stack up behind them. Add the next aligned sign or reception and the queue resumes automatically. A newly obstructed or missing block similarly stops traffic until the block is clear again; ordinary waiting never returns personnel to deployment and has no timeout. Full reception outputs also retain the queue safely. Conflicting directions wait before entering an occupied segment. Merges use admission order. A tightly folded existing layout still carries one journey at a time to avoid trapping full bodies at close hairpins; spread out turns for convoy throughput.

Signs can be rotated at any time, including with a biter inside the sign tile. An approaching biter follows arrow changes while its full body remains outside the sign tile. Once its body enters, it keeps that saved valid direction through the turn regardless of later arrow changes. The next biter follows the current arrow, or waits outside the sign tile if it points toward an invalid path. One biter at a time owns the junction until its full body clears it; a newly chosen direction can start then, without waiting for the previous biter to finish the entire old leg. The saved old lane remains protected until its traveler finishes. Reception uses the same entry reservation to keep simultaneous arrivals from four sides from blocking each other. Signs take selection priority over walking proxies, including in older saves. Mining protection remains active while personnel depend on the junction.


Walking personnel have role-specific body tints. Existing item colors carry over for workers, trainees, and operations specialists; the remaining roles have their own colors. Ordinary managers are violet, while each briefing and courier type has a separate tint. Legacy worker items share the worker's green color. Body tint identifies the role loaded at departure. When a briefing expires, its badge and time bar disappear; reception returns the ordinary manager item. The body retains its departure tint until reception. Native sprite masks provide the color; body details, shadows, and worker helmets remain intact.

Signs and offices on each admitted segment are protected from mining and combat while its personnel depend on them. An occupied Deployment Office cannot be rotated. Reception Offices never rotate; signs remain freely rotatable. The last incoming pavement remains protected until the full biter body clears the turn; older segment reservations are then released. To edit occupied infrastructure, stop loading deployment, extend a waiting lane to reception if necessary, and drain the queue. Automatic pavement follows the edited connections. The signs use four transparent industrial arrow sprites prepared from the supplied artwork. Deployment has four generated office directions and reception has one fixed four-intake sprite. Masters, built-in imagegen prompts, and deterministic exports are kept in `Internal/personnel-routing-art/`.


The original item stays in a saved script inventory throughout transit. Its quality, health, and item metadata are retained; transport does not train personnel. Spoilage clocks continue normally, including while queued at a full output: expired briefings or couriers return their existing spoil result, without renewing the briefing or losing the manager. Briefed managers display a small briefing-material badge (gear, repair pack, form, circuit, or rocket fuel) and a shrinking remaining-time bar above their walking entity. The bar changes from green to amber to red as expiry approaches; the badge and bar disappear when the briefing expires. Save/load retains the overlay and its original spoilage clock. Reception commits the item to an output slot before releasing the proxy and its reservations. A full output retains the item and waits.

If infrastructure supporting the current segment or a proxy is forcibly deleted by another script, the walking proxy is removed and the original item returns to deployment. If deployment is unavailable or full, a **Personnel Recovery Crate** appears nearby and raises an alert. Retrieve its contents manually or with an inserter. If no crate can be placed, cargo remains in saved recovery storage and placement retries every five seconds. Surface deletion/clearing evacuates in-flight personnel to recovery crates at the force's Nauvis spawn. Save/load retains jobs, cargo, and reservations. Field Agents already traveling in a save from before their exclusion are recovered safely during the update.

There are no circuit controls on these offices. Control input with your inserter and drain the output before removing or rebuilding a route. Sign arrows can change during traffic. This system is independent of employment dispatch, Biterport logistics, and passenger trains.
