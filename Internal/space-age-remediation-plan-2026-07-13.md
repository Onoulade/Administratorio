# Administratorio Space Age Remediation Plan

Date: 2026-07-13  
Target branch: `space-age`  
Source assessment: `Internal/space-age-review-2026-07-12.md`

## Implementation status

The code implementation described by this plan was completed on 2026-07-13. Automated content/runtime tests, strict base progression analysis, named Space Age planet import-policy profiles, a real Space Age prototype load, fresh-map initialization, and a 600-tick headless smoke test pass against Factorio 2.0.77.

The remaining release activity is empirical tuning through human campaign play: time-to-bootstrap measurements, subjective failure-streak tolerance, real-base recycler pressure, and final balance adjustments. Those are playtest gates rather than missing code paths.

One engine-driven adjustment was made during implementation: archive recombination accepts two forms at the same quality and requires archival substrate at that same quality. Successful output preserves that quality. Factorio recipes match ingredient quality exactly, so permitting mixed qualities would either stall invisibly or require a custom inventory processor that bypasses normal recipe behavior. Exact matching prevents quality laundering while keeping the building legible and automatable.

## Objective

Bring the Space Age branch from a technically broad first pass to a coherent, locally viable, campaign-ready expansion without adding feature volume indiscriminately.

The plan adopts four fixed planetary identities:

| Planet | Abundant administrative family | Colored family | Gameplay expression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vulcanus | Politician fluid and direct derivatives, especially `lie` and molten promises | Cyan | Abundant propaganda is consumed heavily to arbitrate demolisher territory and certify metallurgy |
| Gleba | Bullshit ore and direct derivatives, especially dubious data, excuses, and refined nonsense | Yellow | Biological overproduction, perishable paperwork, conciliation, and creature intake |
| Fulgora | Redundant rubble and recovered archives | Magenta | Salvage-driven paperwork supply, lossy form recombination, and dry reconstruction media |
| Aquilo | No base administrative abundance; convergence is its identity | Multicolor/solid transfer | Imported planetary paperwork is consolidated, recombined, and faxed without liquid ink |

Nauvis remains the balanced generalist. It can produce every ordinary administrative family but should not match the basic planets' local abundance in their specialties.

The colored identity and the base-resource identity are separate axes. Vulcanus is both cyan and propaganda-rich; Gleba is both yellow and bullshit-rich; Fulgora is both magenta and rubble-rich. A planet's colored forms should not become a universal substitute for its abundant base administrative resource.

## Non-negotiable design rules

1. Vulcanus, Gleba, and Fulgora must support ordinary local production and escape.
2. Imports may still be required for specialist workers, conflict-resolution milestones, unique planetary exports, and optional advanced systems.
3. Aquilo is intentionally import-dependent.
4. No required progression milestone may depend solely on a random roll.
5. Random systems must expose their possible outcomes and must be deterministic in multiplayer/replays.
6. A planet's abundance should solve its own production problems without flattening the other planets' identities.
7. Colored provenance is conserved: no system may create cyan, yellow, or magenta value unless that color exists in its inputs or a valid multicolor carrier.
8. Top-tier charters, territorial deeds, complaint cases, resolved complaints, money, workers, science packs, and unique quest/capstone documents cannot be generated by generic form recycling.
9. Faxing must improve logistics without becoming free document duplication.
10. Space Age cargo rockets must not trigger the base-only victory state.

## Central Fulgora feature: Archive Recombination Bureau

### Concept

Add a Fulgora-built administrative machine with the working name `Archive Recombination Bureau`.

The Bureau accepts two different recyclable forms plus a solid archival substrate. It destroys both forms and attempts to reconstruct one different form. Each valid unordered input pair has two or three possible outputs chosen from the same general progression band. An attempt has a 50% chance to produce exactly one form and a 50% chance to produce no form.

The building is intentionally lossy. Its purpose is to convert surpluses and awkward random archive finds into a more useful portfolio, not to duplicate valuable documents.

It should feel like feeding two unrelated government records into a machine and receiving either a third, legally dubious document or an error message saying both originals have been shredded. As the Doctrine of Administrative Conservation states: paperwork is neither created nor destroyed, except when it very obviously is.

### Why this belongs on Fulgora

- It extends vanilla Fulgora's recycling identity instead of adding a second generic printer.
- It makes redundant rubble and archive recovery mechanically important.
- It gives the Digital Services Bureau a complementary system instead of replacing it.
- It creates a useful global reward that can be shipped to Aquilo.
- It turns random salvage into a planning problem rather than a belt-cluttering pile of finished permits.

### Building behavior

Initial balance targets:

| Property | Starting value |
| --- | --- |
| Inputs per attempt | 2 different eligible forms + 1 archival substrate |
| Attempt duration | 20 seconds |
| Success probability | 50% |
| Successful output | Exactly 1 form |
| Possible outputs per pair | 2 or 3 |
| Energy use | 1 MW |
| Module slots | 0 |
| Allowed effects | None initially |
| Working hours | 24/7 |
| Crafting location | Building recipe crafted on Fulgora |
| Placement | Any non-vacuum surface, including Aquilo |
| Specialist cost | 1 relay clerk in the building recipe |

At these starting values, one machine attempts three cycles per minute, consumes six source forms per minute, and produces an average of 1.5 forms per minute. This is a 75% net form sink before considering candidate usefulness. The machine therefore cannot replace normal printing.

Both source forms and the archival substrate must have the same quality. The output preserves that quality. This prevents quality laundering and matches Factorio's exact-quality recipe semantics. Productivity is disabled. Speed modules should remain disabled until the system is proven not to trivialize paperwork throughput.

Identical input forms are rejected. There is no recipe for `form A + form A`.

### Recommended implementation shape

Use a hybrid native/runtime implementation:

1. Create a dedicated furnace-style entity with three source slots and one result slot.
2. Generate one hidden recipe for every valid unordered pair of recyclable forms.
3. Each hidden recipe consumes the pair plus one `archival-substrate` and has one probabilistic result: a generic hidden `recombination-envelope` at probability 0.5.
4. Track the selected pair and input qualities when the craft begins.
5. When an envelope appears, replace it with exactly one candidate chosen from that pair's output table.
6. If no envelope appears, the attempt has failed and no form is produced.

Using only one probabilistic recipe result guarantees a true 50% chance of either zero or one form. Defining three independent probabilistic form products directly in a recipe would allow multiple outputs and would not produce a real 50% success rate.

If furnace auto-selection cannot reliably preserve the pair through completion, fall back to a bounded runtime processor in `scripts/archive_recombination.lua`. Do not accept independent-result probability as a shortcut.

The runtime portion should use a deterministic hash of force index, building unit number, sorted input names, and completed-attempt count. This is random-looking, save-stable, and multiplayer deterministic. Reloading a save must not reroll an attempt.

### Paperwork taxonomy

Create a shared pure-Lua registry, tentatively `prototypes/shared/paperwork_taxonomy.lua`, also usable from control-stage code and tests.

Every recyclable document receives:

- `rank`: progression band from 0 through 4;
- `family`: blank, permit, work-order, compliance, management, or chromatic;
- `colors`: none, cyan, yellow, magenta, or a set of colors;
- `unlock_technology`: technology required before the item may be generated;
- `recyclable_input`: whether the Bureau accepts it;
- `generatable_output`: whether the Bureau may produce it;
- `restricted`: true for documents that must never enter generic recombination.

Recommended initial bands:

| Rank | Examples | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0: clerical | blank form, blank approval, blank directive, basic carbon certificate, provisional approval, simple drafts, base work order | Bootstrap and basic automation |
| 1: operational | safety waiver, construction permit, research approval, safety/construction/research work orders, transit authorization, Form 27B/6 | Regular factory construction and operation |
| 2: departmental | verbal approval, verbal work order, environmental report, verified carbon certificate, chemical work order, planet-local blank colored forms | Chemical/midgame and planet administration |
| 3: executive | written proposal/approval/work order, radiological work order, white paper, selected durable planet certificates, bicolor forms | Late administration and cross-planet work |
| 4: convergence | selected non-capstone multicolor forms and high administrative documents | Aquilo-era convergence only |

The exact membership must be reviewed against actual unlocks before implementation. Complaint tickets, filings, cases, briefs, resolved complaints, OSHA violations, tourist packages, territorial deeds, industrial/off-world charters, unified operations charters, promethium charters, and workforce items are excluded from generic recombination.

Start with 24-32 deliberately selected recyclable documents, keeping the generated unordered-pair count at or below 496. “Any combination” means every pair within this declared recyclable set works; exclusions must be thematic/progression decisions, not unexplained holes. Measure prototype load time before expanding the set.

### Candidate rules

For inputs A and B:

1. Sort the pair by internal name so `A+B` and `B+A` are the same combination.
2. Output may not be A or B; it must be a third document.
3. Output rank must be at least `min(rankA, rankB)`.
4. Output rank must be no greater than `min(max(rankA, rankB), min(rankA, rankB) + 1)`.
5. The output's colors must be a subset of the union of the input colors.
6. A bicolor output requires those two colors across the inputs.
7. A tricolor output requires one bicolor input plus the missing single color, or another input union that genuinely contains all three colors.
8. The force must have researched the candidate's unlock technology.
9. Restricted documents are never candidates.
10. Prefer candidates in a different family from both inputs so the result feels transformed rather than renamed.

Rule 4 is the main anti-tier-jump protection. Feeding one rank-4 form and one rank-0 form can produce only rank 0 or 1. Two near-top forms are required to remain near the top.

Example:

- `carbon-offset-certificate-basic + work-order` may produce `blank-approval`, `provisional-approval`, or `safety-waiver-draft`.
- It may not produce a written management approval, radiological order, bicolor form, or planetary charter.

For three candidates, start with conditional weights of 45%, 35%, and 20% after the 50% success roll. Their total per-attempt probabilities are therefore 22.5%, 17.5%, and 10%. For two candidates, start with 60% and 40%, or 30% and 20% per attempt.

Generate and freeze the pair-to-candidate table from the taxonomy. Add a test requiring every valid pair to have exactly two or three candidates. If a pair cannot satisfy the rules, either expand the same-tier candidate pool or mark one of its documents non-recyclable; do not silently allow a tier/color violation.

### Player information and automation

The building GUI and Factoriopedia entry must show:

- the two accepted input forms;
- the two or three possible outputs;
- conditional candidate weights;
- overall 50% success chance;
- current progress;
- the result of the last attempt;
- why a pair is invalid;
- whether a candidate is unavailable because its technology is locked.

The randomness should be in the result, not in understanding the rules.

Circuit outputs:

- input-ready;
- valid-pair;
- working;
- success count;
- failure count;
- output blocked.

Do not add circuit-controlled exact-output selection in the first pass. That would turn the machine into a complicated deterministic printer and erase the premise.

## Fulgora archive supply

### Choose Old Archives from scrap, not a new ore

Use `old-archive` as a secondary recycling product of ordinary Fulgora scrap. Do not add a new mineable ore in the first implementation.

Reasons:

- Fulgora's existing identity is salvage, not conventional mining.
- A new ore adds map-generation and starting-area balance work without creating a better loop.
- Scrap-to-archive-to-form is immediately legible.
- It removes the previous temptation to scatter completed forms directly across scrap outputs.

A separate archive deposit can remain a future option only if real-map tests show scrap cannot provide a stable bootstrap.

### Replace direct finished-document scrap drops

Current scrap additions include toner, rubble, documentation, form stock, blank magenta forms, and four completed permits. Replace the broad finished-document drops with a narrower salvage pool:

- keep redundant rubble as the abundant primary output;
- keep or reduce charged toner;
- add `old-archive` at a low rate;
- remove completed Fulgora permits from direct scrap output;
- remove or sharply reduce direct signal-form stock and blank-magenta-form drops;
- let the Digital Services Bureau and Chromatic Printer finish the recovered material.

Starting tuning range:

| Scrap addition | Initial target |
| --- | --- |
| Redundant rubble | 0.5-0.7 expected per scrap |
| Charged toner | 0.08-0.15 expected per scrap |
| Old archive | 4-8% chance per scrap |
| Completed forms | None, or one rare jackpot below 0.5% |

These are prototype values, not release values. Tune them against a real Fulgora base and recycler output-slot pressure.

### Recycling Old Archives

Old archives are recycled again in a standard Recycler. The result pool contains only low/ordinary paperwork needed to start Fulgora administration, plus redundant rubble.

Recommended first pool:

- blank form;
- blank approval;
- basic carbon-offset certificate;
- provisional approval;
- work order;
- safety-waiver draft;
- construction-permit draft;
- research-grant approval.

Use independent low-probability products deliberately here. An old archive represents a bundle and may reasonably contain more than one document.

Starting target:

- 2 redundant rubble guaranteed;
- six to eight form products around 8-12% each;
- approximately 0.7 expected forms per old archive;
- roughly 50-60% chance of at least one form;
- occasional multiple-form archive finds are allowed and should feel like a jackpot.

Old archives supply the initial random portfolio. The Archive Recombination Bureau converts surplus forms into other same-band possibilities. Neither system alone is allowed to become a mandatory roulette gate.

### Archival substrate

Add `archival-substrate`, a dry standardized reconstruction medium made primarily from Fulgora's abundant rubble:

- redundant rubble;
- charged toner;
- a small amount of useless documentation or recovered paper.

It should be produced deterministically in the Digital Services Bureau. This gives the Bureau an important Fulgora/export role and avoids making fax supply depend on old-archive RNG.

Archival substrate is consumed by:

- Archive Recombination Bureau attempts;
- fax reconstruction;
- selected Fulgora-local deterministic paperwork shortcuts.

Failed recombination attempts may produce `archive-residue`. Four residue can be reprocessed into one archival substrate. This is optional loss mitigation, not a guaranteed form pity system.

## Fulgora ordinary-production repair

The random archive system is not sufficient by itself for regular factory throughput. Add deterministic local routes for the few foundational inputs that otherwise pull Nauvis resources.

Required Fulgora recipes:

1. `liquid-black-ink-fulgora`
   - charged toner plus a local fluid/electromagnetic ingredient;
   - no coal.
2. `dubious-data-recovery-fulgora`
   - old archives or archival substrate plus redundant rubble;
   - no bullshit ore.
3. `basic-excuse-recovery-fulgora`
   - recovered data and archive material;
   - deterministic and suitable for ordinary building paperwork.
4. `carbon-offset-certificate-basic-fulgora`
   - blank form plus solid fuel/electromagnetic evidence and archive material;
   - no coal.
5. `electromagnetic-lubricant` or an equivalent Fulgora electric-engine route
   - electrolyte/holmium solution and recycled hydrocarbon material;
   - no crude oil import.
6. A Fulgora-local rocket-silo authorization
   - replace taxpayer-money dependence with Fulgora digital permits and a substantial redundant-rubble/archive cost.

Use the Digital Services Bureau for deterministic recovery recipes and the Archive Recombination Bureau for lossy portfolio conversion. Their roles must remain distinct.

Acceptance targets:

- AM2 requires no ordinary imports.
- Oil refinery requires no ordinary imports.
- Chemical plant may require its deliberate specialist worker but no coal/bullshit import.
- Rocket silo and 100 rocket parts require no coal, crude oil, bullshit ore, politician fluid, or taxpayer-money import.

## Aquilo integration

### Replace liquid-ink fax reconstruction

Aquilo's fiction is that liquid ink freezes. Fax reconstruction therefore must not consume liquid black, cyan, yellow, or magenta ink.

New reconstruction costs:

| Document type | Destination cost |
| --- | --- |
| Black paperwork | 1 thermal-transfer sheet + 1 archival substrate |
| Single-color paperwork | 1 thermal-transfer sheet + 1 archival substrate + 1 chroma-ribbon charge |
| Bicolor paperwork | 1 thermal-transfer sheet + 2 archival substrate + 1 chroma-ribbon charge |
| Tricolor/capstone faxable paperwork | 2 thermal-transfer sheets + 2 archival substrate + 2 chroma-ribbon charges |

Rename or split `composite-chroma-ribbon` into a stackable consumable charge if needed. Producing a ribbon from imported cyan, yellow, and magenta forms should yield multiple charges—start around 8-12—so physical color imports are compressed rather than repeated one-for-one.

This preserves three costs:

- the source document is consumed by the emitter;
- Fulgora exports dry archival substrate;
- earlier planets periodically export colored paperwork to make ribbon charges.

Faxing is therefore convenient and compressive, not free.

### Archive Recombination Bureau on Aquilo

The Bureau has no fluid inputs and can operate on Aquilo. It gives the player a lossy way to convert surplus imported/faxed forms into other same-tier forms.

It is a support tool, not the only Aquilo progression route. Critical Aquilo production uses deterministic Laser Printer and fax recipes. A player must never be forced to roll repeatedly for the one cryogenic form needed to continue.

### Laser Printer cleanup

- Remove `fax-reconstruction` from the Laser Printer if fax completion remains owned by the Exchange runtime.
- Alternatively, make reconstruction recipes solid-only and intentionally available in both machines.
- Keep the Laser Printer focused on thermal sheets, ribbon charges, multicolor forms, and fast final printing.

## Vulcanus abundance repair

Vulcanus already has the strongest local route. Refocus it explicitly around politician-fluid derivatives.

Changes:

1. Treat `lie`, `molten-promises`, and related fluids as the planet's abundant administrative family.
2. Increase local production enough that the player is encouraged to build a dedicated propaganda fluid industry.
3. Make Territorial Arbitration consume these fluids heavily and continuously.
4. Scale total arbitration demand by demolisher size/territory value.
5. Keep the territorial deed as the conflict-resolution milestone allowed to gate the local rocket silo.
6. Avoid letting cheap lies replace cyan certification; both loops should be necessary.

Desired experience:

- cyan paperwork certifies industrial work;
- abundant propaganda funds and legally overwhelms the demolisher dispute;
- a territorial deed replaces military victory;
- the player exports lie/propaganda derivatives because Vulcanus is unusually efficient at producing them.

Acceptance targets:

- no ordinary production import except explicitly allowed specialist/conflict items;
- one normal starting-area demolisher can be arbitrated without an excessive idle grind;
- the deed requirement is communicated before landing and in the rocket-silo chain.

## Gleba abundance repair

Gleba should produce almost embarrassingly large quantities of bullshit ore and its immediate derivatives.

Changes:

1. Increase or accelerate `amber-sap-nonsense-seeding` after initial map testing.
2. Add efficient Gleba-local routes from bullshit ore/amber sap to:
   - dubious data;
   - basic and good excuses;
   - refined nonsense;
   - filler documentation needed by ordinary rocket paperwork.
3. Replace bulk politician-fluid requirements in the launch chain with local bullshit derivatives and yellow paperwork.
4. Replace imported redundant rubble in ordinary production with a Gleba-local disposable-document or composted-record derivative.
5. Keep yellow-form spoilage; do not turn bullshit abundance into exemption from biological paperwork.
6. Keep the conciliation-officer worker as an allowed specialist import if desired, but document it in the pre-launch manifest.
7. Replace taxpayer money in the ordinary Gleba rocket-silo authorization with a local conciliation/public-interest authorization.

Desired experience:

- the player has too much low-quality administrative material;
- spoilage and conciliation, not raw supply, are the logistics challenge;
- bullshit derivatives feed spores, excuses, and rocket administration;
- higher-quality yellow paperwork still requires deliberate biological handling.

Acceptance targets:

- AM2 is fully local.
- Chemical plant requires at most its deliberate specialist worker.
- Biochamber requires at most its deliberate specialist worker, not bulk politician fluid/rubble.
- Rocket silo and 100 rocket parts require no bulk politician fluid, redundant rubble, or taxpayer-money imports.

## Balance safeguards

### Randomness

- 50% is the initial Bureau success rate.
- Track the longest failure streak in playtests.
- Do not add a guaranteed-form pity system unless streaks make the feature unpleasant.
- Archive residue may soften failures but cannot be redeemed directly for a chosen form.
- Candidate mappings are visible to the player.

### Throughput

- One Bureau starts at 1.5 expected forms per minute.
- No productivity.
- No speed modules in the first pass.
- Old Archives provide bootstrap supply; deterministic local recipes provide critical throughput.
- The Bureau is never the most efficient way to produce a form already available through its proper chain.

### Tier protection

- No output above the lower input's rank plus one.
- No output above the higher input's rank.
- No restricted document generation.
- No unresearched document generation.
- No new color generation.

### Economic identity

- Vulcanus pays with propaganda fluid derivatives.
- Gleba pays with abundant low-quality nonsense and spoilage management.
- Fulgora pays with rubble, archive substrate, and destroyed surplus forms.
- Aquilo pays with imports, thermal media, and convergence infrastructure.

## Test plan

### Taxonomy and pair generation

- every eligible unordered pair is generated once;
- identical pairs are absent;
- every pair has two or three candidates;
- output differs from both inputs;
- rank rules hold;
- color-union rules hold;
- restricted documents never appear;
- locked documents are filtered until their technology is researched;
- the mapping is stable across loads.

### Runtime recombination

- exactly two forms and one substrate are consumed;
- success produces exactly one form;
- failure produces zero forms;
- empirical success rate is within tolerance over a large deterministic sample;
- candidate weights are within tolerance;
- input order does not affect the pool;
- mixed input qualities are rejected without consuming anything;
- matching input and substrate quality is preserved in the output;
- save/reload does not reroll an in-progress attempt;
- multiplayer event order produces the same result;
- blocked output pauses safely;
- invalid pairs show a clear status and consume nothing;
- entity removal returns or spills inventories correctly;
- configuration changes rebuild state without deleting forms.

### Scrap and archive supply

- scrap output slots remain sufficient;
- completed permits no longer flood recycler outputs;
- Old Archives appear at the configured rate;
- Old Archive recycling has the intended expected yield;
- first Fulgora bootstrap does not depend on a single lucky archive;
- deterministic foundational recipes prevent RNG progression locks.

### Aquilo and fax

- Fax Exchange works on Aquilo when heated/powered as intended;
- Laser Printer categories match recipes it can actually craft;
- colored faxing consumes solid ribbon charges;

Exit criterion: players can understand each planet's loop in-game and no single specialist building erases the core paperwork economy.

### Phase 7: maintainability and release validation

1. Split oversized Space Age recipe and fax/runtime files along the boundaries identified in the review.
2. Add Lua linting.
3. Remove tracked cache artifacts.
4. Run multiplayer, migration, and performance smoke tests.
5. Complete a full campaign and update implemented-state documentation from the shipped code.

Exit criterion: all release acceptance criteria from the assessment are satisfied.

## Decisions made by this plan

- Use Old Archives as a scrap byproduct, not a new ore, for the first implementation.
- Make redundant rubble the scalable Fulgora resource and archival substrate its export derivative.
- Add a lossy two-form Archive Recombination Bureau with a true 50% zero-or-one-form outcome.
- Keep random recombination out of mandatory progression by providing deterministic foundational recipes.
- Conserve tier and color in all recombination outcomes.
- Make the Bureau globally placeable and dry so it works on Aquilo.
- Replace Aquilo liquid-ink fax costs with Fulgora archival substrate and solid chroma charges.
- Give each basic planet a planet-specific rocket authorization instead of recreating Nauvis taxpayer finance.
- Preserve specialist/conflict imports where they create preparation or gameplay rather than ordinary-production chores.

## Final design target

The intended interplanetary economy is:

1. Vulcanus exports propaganda derivatives, cyan certification, and legally acquired territory.
2. Gleba exports abundant nonsense, yellow biological paperwork, and conciliation products.
3. Fulgora exports rubble-derived archival substrate, magenta digital paperwork, and the Archive Recombination Bureau.
4. Aquilo imports all three administrative traditions, compresses their colors into solid media, and turns the fax network into the final logistics reward.

Every planet is locally viable. No planet is administratively self-sufficient in every dimension. The player solves ordinary production locally, then builds an interplanetary paperwork economy because it is useful and expressive—not because a hidden recipe demanded three thousand units of imported coal and ruined everyone's contour.
